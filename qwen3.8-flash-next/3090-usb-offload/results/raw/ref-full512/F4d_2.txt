Elias had lived with the sea for forty years, his skin weathered like driftwood and his eyes the color of a stormy horizon. The lighthouse, a solitary sentinel on the jagged cliffs of Cape Vane, was his kingdom. Every evening, as the sun bled into the Atlantic, he climbed the spiral stairs, his joints creaking in harmony with the wind.

He polished the great lens until it gleamed like a diamond, then lit the flame. The beam swept across the dark waters, a rhythmic heartbeat in the chaos of the night. Ships passed, their horns lowing like distant whales, acknowledging his silent vigil. Elias never spoke to them; he simply watched, ensuring they found their way home.

One night, a violent gale battered the tower. Rain lashed against the glass, and the sea roared like a wounded beast. Elias stood firm, his hand steady on the railing. He wasn’t afraid of the storm; he was part of it. As dawn broke, painting the sky in hues of violet and gold, the sea calmed. A small fishing boat, battered but intact, limped into the harbor. The captain waved up at the tower. Elias waved back, a faint smile touching his lips. He had done his job. The light had held.
