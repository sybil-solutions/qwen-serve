Elias had lived with the sea for forty years, his skin weathered like driftwood and his eyes the color of a stormy horizon. The lighthouse, a solitary sentinel on the jagged cliffs of Cape Vane, was his kingdom. Every evening, as the sun bled into the Atlantic, he climbed the spiral stairs, his boots echoing against the cold stone.

The light was not just a duty; it was a promise. A promise to the sailors navigating the treacherous shoals, a promise that someone was watching, that they were not alone in the vast, indifferent dark.

One night, a violent gale battered the tower. Rain lashed against the glass like shrapnel. Elias polished the lens, his hands steady despite the trembling walls. He watched the beam sweep across the churning waves, a golden finger pointing toward safety.

Then, he saw it—a small fishing boat, capsized and drifting toward the rocks. Without hesitation, Elias grabbed his flare gun and ran to the gallery. He fired three bright red stars into the sky, guiding the rescue team. As the boat was pulled from the abyss, Elias smiled, the wind tearing at his coat. He was just a keeper, but tonight, he had kept more than a light; he had kept a life.
