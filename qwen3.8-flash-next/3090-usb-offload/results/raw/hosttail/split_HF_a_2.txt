Elias had not spoken a word in three years. The lighthouse, perched on the jagged teeth of the northern coast, demanded silence as much as it demanded vigilance. Every evening, as the sun bled into the horizon, he climbed the spiral stairs, his boots echoing against the cold stone. The great lens, a beast of glass and brass, waited for him.

He polished it with a reverence usually reserved for religious icons. When the darkness finally swallowed the sea, he lit the flame. The beam swept out, a golden finger pointing into the void, cutting through the fog that clung to the rocks like a shroud. Ships passed, their horns low and mournful, acknowledging his presence without seeing him.

One stormy night, the wind howled like a wounded animal. Elias watched the waves crash against the base, white foam exploding against the black water. A small fishing boat, battered and listing, struggled against the current. Elias didn’t think; he acted. He grabbed his oilskin coat and descended to the shore, shouting into the gale, his voice raw and unused. He hauled the exhausted fisherman up the slippery rocks, saving him from the churning sea.

Back in the tower, the fisherman thanked him, tears mixing with rain on his face. Elias simply nodded, returning to his post. The light continued its steady rotation, a silent promise kept in the dark.
